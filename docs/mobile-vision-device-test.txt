MOBILE MACHINE VISION: DEVICE TEST CHECKLIST (Android first, then iPhone)

Why this exists: the camera games (Vision Play, Pop Quiz Toss, the Driver's Ed widget) run in a WebView inside the
app. Emulation in a browser can prove the code, the files and the geometry. It cannot prove the camera permission
prompt, the phone's GPU, memory pressure or heat. Those need a real phone, and this is how to test one and what to
send back. Nothing here records pictures or audio. Every report is text: numbers, error names and timings.

WHAT IS ALREADY CHECKED WITHOUT A PHONE (scripts/mobile_vision_check.mjs)
  Run: scripts/mobile_vision_fixtures.sh   (once; needs ffmpeg)
       node scripts/mobile_vision_check.mjs [--base https://salareen.com]
  It drives Chromium with Pixel 7 and iPhone 14 profiles and a fake camera, plus WebKit (the iPhone engine), and checks:
    - the MediaPipe files are served with the right type and a cache lifetime
    - the library, wasm and models load and find a face and two hands (WebKit and Chromium)
    - face and hands are found from the fake camera on both phone profiles
    - overlays line up with the video at four phone sizes, portrait and landscape, mirrored (landmark mapping error in px,
      plus the overlay centre against the face found in a screenshot)
    - Pop Quiz Toss: the hand on the left of the screen throws at the left answer, the right hand at the right
    - the page's own camera check reports a healthy phone and a blocked camera correctly
    - /diag/vision runs end to end and its report has no tokens or emails
    - cold and warm model load on throttled 4G with a 4x slower CPU (the second visit must download nothing)
  Limits: emulation uses software graphics, so speeds are not phone speeds. WebKit cannot take a fake camera, so the iPhone
  engine is tested at library level on a photo, not on a live camera.

ANDROID (the first target)

A. Before you start
  1. Install the build:   adb install -r salareen-<version>-<code>-release.apk
     Check the version: Settings > About (bottom of the app). It must match the build you were sent.
  2. Note the device: adb shell getprop ro.product.model ; adb shell getprop ro.build.version.release
  3. Note the WebView in use (this decides which Chrome engine runs the games):
       adb shell dumpsys webviewupdate | head -20
  4. Clear the log so the test starts clean:   adb logcat -c   and   adb logcat -b events -c
  5. Optional, to test the "camera blocked" path later:
       adb shell pm revoke com.aiclassroom.app android.permission.CAMERA     (undo with: pm grant ... CAMERA)

B. The in-app camera check (do this first, 1 minute)
  1. Settings > Camera check > Run camera check. (Or open any camera game and tap "Camera check" at the top right.)
  2. Tap Start the check. Tap Allow if the phone asks. Keep your face and one open hand in view for about 12 seconds.
  3. Read the result at the top: "Looks good", "Works, with warnings" or "Problem found", with advice lines.
  4. Tap Share report (top of the screen) and send it to us. The report is text only. You can read it first.
     If you prefer, tap Send to Salareen: it opens Report a bug with the camera diagnostics ready, and a Preview
     button shows exactly what would be sent. Nothing is sent until you tap Send.
  What the report contains: device and WebView version, cores and memory, graphics chip, permissions, camera size and
  fps, how long each model took to load and whether the GPU or the CPU was used (and why it fell back), detection
  counts and speed (median and 95th percentile per frame), and the last 200 log lines.

C. Game checks (about 5 minutes)
  Vision Play (Machine vision):
    1. Open Vision Play. The tutorial must appear first. Tap Allow for the camera. Status says "Face + two hands ready".
    2. Hold up one hand, then two. The readout shows Hands: 1, then Hands: 2. The face contour follows your face.
    3. Turn the phone to landscape and back. The overlay stays on your face and hands (no offset, no mirror flip).
    4. Gold body outline: step back so your shoulders show. A soft gold line follows your body.
  Pop Quiz Toss:
    1. Start the game, read the tutorial. Hold your LEFT hand up on the left, grab the ball (close the fist), release.
       It must go to the LEFT answer. Repeat with the RIGHT hand: it must go to the RIGHT answer.
    2. Try holding the phone with the camera low. Both hands must still be found when you stand back a little.
    3. A wrong throw never curves. Each question allows at least 10 seconds.
  Driver's Ed demo (/demo/drivers-ed in the app):
    1. The tutorial shows first, then the intro video. One small movable panel sits at the top right: status dot,
       camera preview, mic, minimize, menu. A signed-in learner sees no countdown timer.
    2. The face outline in the panel sits on your face.
    3. Pick a voice in the account menu. The narration uses that voice.
  Stress (optional): leave a game running for 5 minutes. Note whether the phone gets hot or the game slows.

D. If something looks wrong, collect this (all text, no images)
  Run these on the computer with the phone connected by USB (enable Developer options and USB debugging first):
    adb devices
    adb logcat -v time chromium:V cr_*:V *:S                                  (live Chromium and WebView messages)
    adb logcat -v time | grep -iE "camera|webview|chromium|mediapipe|OOM|lowmemory|ANR"
    adb logcat -b events -d | grep -iE "am_kill|am_low_memory|am_proc_died"    (was the app killed for memory?)
    adb shell dumpsys media.camera | head -80                                 (is another app holding the camera?)
    adb shell dumpsys meminfo com.aiclassroom.app                             (run while the game is open)
    adb shell dumpsys gfxinfo com.aiclassroom.app                             (frame timing and janky frames)
    adb shell dumpsys package com.aiclassroom.app | grep -iE "permission|CAMERA|RECORD_AUDIO"
    adb shell dumpsys thermalservice | head -30 ; adb shell dumpsys battery   (heat and charge)
    adb bugreport salareen-bugreport.zip                                      (everything; large; only if we ask)
  Screen recording (shows what you see, including the camera picture of you; only send it if you are comfortable):
    adb shell screenrecord --time-limit 60 /sdcard/salareen-test.mp4 ; adb pull /sdcard/salareen-test.mp4

E. Looking inside the WebView from a computer (chrome://inspect)
  1. In the app: Settings > About, tap the version line 7 quick times. A message says Debug mode is on.
     (Tap it 7 times again to turn it off. Debug mode also logs more detail.)
  2. Phone connected by USB with USB debugging on. On the computer open Chrome and go to chrome://inspect/#devices.
  3. Find "Salareen" under your phone and click inspect on the page. Use the Console and Network tabs.
  4. In the Console, type   __gsDiag.text()   to print the same report the app shares, or   __gsDiag.report()   for the data.
     __gsVision.getVisionDiag() shows which delegate (GPU or CPU) each model loaded with.

F. What to send back
  - The shared report text (B.4), the phone model and Android version, and the app version.
  - Which step failed (for example "Pop Quiz Toss, step 1: right hand threw left") and what you saw.
  - If the app closed or the screen went blank: the "am_kill" lines from D and the dumpsys meminfo output.
  - Do not send pictures of other people. Do not send your password or account details; the report redacts tokens and emails,
    but read it before you send it.

IPHONE

A. Before you start
  - Install the TestFlight or ad hoc build. Note the iOS version (Settings > General > About) and the model.
  - Settings > Salareen: Camera and Microphone must be on. If you tapped Don't Allow earlier, turn them on here.
B. In-app camera check: same as Android B (Settings > Camera check). Tap Share report: the iOS share sheet opens;
   choose Mail or Messages, or Copy.
C. Game checks: same as Android C. Extra for iPhone:
    - The first time, iOS asks for the camera once. The page should not ask a second time.
    - The video must stay inline (not jump to full screen).
    - Low Power Mode lowers the camera frame rate: note whether Low Power Mode was on.
D. Looking inside from a Mac (Safari Web Inspector)
    1. iPhone: Settings > Safari > Advanced > Web Inspector on. In the app turn on Debug mode (tap the version 7 times).
    2. Mac: Safari > Settings > Advanced > Show Develop menu. Connect the phone by cable.
    3. Safari > Develop > [your iPhone] > the Salareen page. Console: __gsDiag.text()
E. What to send back: as Android F. If the page went white and came back, the report shows
   "WebView content process terminated; reloading": that means iOS ran the page out of memory.

KNOWN LIMITS AND WHAT A DEVICE TEST CONFIRMS THAT EMULATION CANNOT
  - The camera permission prompt and the in-app WebView grant (Android WebChromeClient, iOS WKWebView).
  - Real GPU speed. The check falls back to the CPU if the GPU model cannot start, and says so.
  - Memory pressure on phones with 2 to 4 GB. Phones with 2 GB or less skip the optional gold body outline to protect the page.
  - Heat and battery during long play.
  - The model files (about 21 MB) are stored on the phone after the first load (Cache Storage, then IndexedDB). The first game
    launch on mobile data downloads them once; later launches download nothing.
