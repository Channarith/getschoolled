LESSON: Driver's License Permit Test Prep
LANGUAGE: en

SLIDE 1 | Welcome to the Permit Test
The driver's license permit test evaluates your knowledge of traffic laws, road signs,
and safe driving practices. Most states require a written knowledge test before issuing
a learner's permit. Questions come directly from your state's driver's handbook. This
lesson covers the core topics tested in nearly every U.S. state.
NARRATION: The permit test is based entirely on your state's driver's handbook; study
it alongside this lesson for state-specific rules.

SLIDE 2 | Rules of the Road - Right of Way
Right of way determines who goes first at intersections and other conflict points. At
an uncontrolled intersection, yield to traffic already in the intersection and to
vehicles on your right. At a four-way stop, the first vehicle to arrive goes first;
if tied, yield to the driver on your right. Always yield to emergency vehicles with
lights and sirens activated by pulling to the right and stopping.
NARRATION: When in doubt, yield; it is better to give up your right of way than to
cause a collision.

SLIDE 3 | Yielding and Merging
Yield signs require you to slow and let crossing or merging traffic go first. When
entering a highway from an on-ramp, yield to highway traffic and accelerate to match
the flow of traffic before merging. When a lane ends and requires merging, the
zipper method (alternate one car at a time) is the safe and courteous technique.
Never force your way into traffic.
NARRATION: Merging requires adjusting your speed to match traffic, not stopping on
the on-ramp unless absolutely necessary.

SLIDE 4 | Traffic Signs - Regulatory Signs
Regulatory signs have a white background and tell you what you must or must not do.
Examples include Stop (red octagon), Yield (red and white triangle), Speed Limit
(white rectangle), Do Not Enter (red circle with white bar), One Way (black and
white), and No U-Turn. These signs are legally enforceable; violating them can result
in citations and accidents.
NARRATION: Red means stop or prohibition; a red octagon is always a stop sign, and
a red circle with a slash means the action is forbidden.

SLIDE 5 | Traffic Signs - Warning Signs
Warning signs have a yellow background with black symbols and alert you to hazards
ahead. Common types include curve ahead, pedestrian crossing, school zone, railroad
crossing, slippery when wet, and merging traffic. Diamond-shaped warning signs are
the most common. They advise you to slow down and proceed with caution, not
necessarily to stop.
NARRATION: Yellow diamond signs warn of upcoming hazards; slow down and be prepared
to react when you see them.

SLIDE 6 | Traffic Signs - Guide Signs
Guide signs provide directional and distance information. Green rectangular signs
are used on highways and streets for route numbers and exit information. Blue signs
indicate services such as fuel, food, and lodging. Brown signs indicate recreational
areas, parks, and historic sites. White signs with black text show route markers and
street names.
NARRATION: Green signs guide you to destinations; blue signs show nearby services
at the next exit.

SLIDE 7 | Traffic Signals and Pavement Markings
A green light means proceed if it is safe; a yellow (amber) light means the signal
is changing to red, so stop if you can safely do so. A red light means stop completely
before the stop line. A flashing red means treat it as a stop sign; a flashing yellow
means slow and proceed with caution. White pavement lines separate traffic traveling
the same direction; yellow lines separate opposing traffic.
NARRATION: Yellow lines separate opposing traffic; white lines separate lanes moving
in the same direction.

SLIDE 8 | Speed Limits and Adjusting for Conditions
Posted speed limits are maximums for ideal conditions. You must drive more slowly
when roads are wet, icy, foggy, or under construction. Common default speed limits
include 25 mph in residential areas, 35 mph on arterial roads, and 65-70 mph on
interstate highways (varies by state). Never drive so fast that you cannot stop
within your visible distance ahead.
NARRATION: The speed limit is the maximum, not a target; always slow for weather,
construction, and heavy traffic.

SLIDE 9 | Following Distance and the 3-Second Rule
Maintain a following distance of at least three seconds behind the vehicle ahead.
To measure: pick a fixed object, note when the vehicle ahead passes it, then count
seconds until you pass the same object. Increase to four or more seconds in rain, fog,
or heavy traffic. Large trucks and motorcycles need extra following distance. Tailgating
(following too closely) is a leading cause of rear-end collisions.
NARRATION: The three-second rule gives you reaction time; poor weather means you need
even more space between you and the car ahead.

SLIDE 10 | Lane Changes and Passing Safely
Before changing lanes: check mirrors, signal your intention, check the blind spot by
looking over your shoulder, then move smoothly. Never change lanes in an intersection.
Passing on two-lane roads requires a dashed yellow center line; never pass on a solid
yellow line on your side. Only pass when you can see clearly and have enough room to
complete the pass and return to your lane safely.
NARRATION: Always signal before changing lanes; check your blind spot every time
because mirrors have dead zones.

SLIDE 11 | Turning Rules at Intersections
Right turns: turn from the right lane into the right lane. Left turns: turn from the
left lane into the left lane. Signal at least 100 feet before turning. Come to a
complete stop at a red light before turning right on red (unless prohibited by a sign).
Yield to pedestrians and oncoming traffic before turning left. U-turns are illegal
where posted, in intersections with no U-turn signs, and in business districts in
some states.
NARRATION: Stay in your lane throughout the turn; turning too wide or cutting the
corner are common test questions and real driving errors.

SLIDE 12 | Roundabout Navigation
Roundabouts are circular intersections where traffic flows counterclockwise. Yield
to traffic already in the roundabout before entering. Choose the correct lane before
entering based on your exit. Do not stop inside the roundabout. Signal when exiting
but not when entering. Roundabouts reduce severe crashes because there are no
head-on or high-speed right-angle collisions.
NARRATION: Yield before entering the roundabout, then flow with traffic; exiting
is the only time you signal in a roundabout.

SLIDE 13 | Parking Rules
Parallel parking requires positioning your vehicle parallel to the curb, within 12-18
inches of it. Perpendicular parking uses spaces at 90 degrees to a curb or wall.
Angle parking is similar at 30-60 degrees. Never park within 15 feet of a fire hydrant,
20 feet of a crosswalk at an intersection, or 30 feet of a stop sign. Do not block
driveways, fire lanes, or handicapped spaces without a valid placard.
NARRATION: Fire hydrant clearance is 15 feet; blocking hydrants and fire lanes is
both illegal and dangerous.

SLIDE 14 | School Zones and School Buses
School zones typically have reduced speed limits of 15-25 mph when children are
present. Obey school zone speed limits during posted hours even if you see no children.
When a school bus stops and displays flashing red lights and a stop arm, all traffic
in both directions on an undivided road must stop at least 20 feet away. Traffic may
proceed after the bus retracts its stop arm and the lights stop flashing.
NARRATION: Failing to stop for a school bus is one of the most serious moving violations
and carries heavy fines and license penalties.

SLIDE 15 | Railroad Crossings
Never cross railroad tracks when lights are flashing or the gate is lowering. Stop at
least 15 feet from the nearest rail. Do not proceed until the train has fully passed
and signals have stopped. Never race a train to a crossing. If your vehicle stalls on
tracks, get out immediately and move away from the tracks at a 45-degree angle in
the direction the train is coming from to avoid debris.
NARRATION: A train cannot stop quickly; if your car is on the tracks, your safety
is more important than the vehicle.

SLIDE 16 | Sharing the Road with Large Trucks
Large trucks have wide turning radii, long stopping distances, and large blind spots
called "no-zones" on the front, rear, and both sides. Never cut in front of a truck
and brake suddenly. Avoid lingering alongside a truck in its blind spot. When a truck
is turning right, it may swing wide to the left first; do not squeeze between the truck
and the curb. Give trucks extra space in all directions.
NARRATION: If you cannot see the truck driver's mirrors, the driver cannot see you;
stay out of the no-zones.

SLIDE 17 | Sharing the Road with Motorcycles, Cyclists, and Pedestrians
Motorcycles are easily hidden in blind spots and are vulnerable in crashes. Give them
a full lane of space. Bicyclists may legally use the road and should be passed with at
least three feet of clearance. Pedestrians always have the right of way in marked or
unmarked crosswalks. At driveways and parking lots, yield to pedestrians before
entering the roadway. Never block a crosswalk when stopped at a red light.
NARRATION: Vulnerable road users depend on drivers to notice them; check blind spots
twice around motorcycles and give cyclists the full three-foot buffer.

SLIDE 18 | Driving Under the Influence
The legal blood alcohol concentration (BAC) limit is 0.08% for adults in all U.S.
states. For commercial drivers, the limit is 0.04%. For drivers under 21, most states
enforce zero-tolerance laws (0.00-0.02%). DUI (driving under the influence) applies
to alcohol, illegal drugs, and prescription medications that impair driving. Penalties
include fines, license suspension, mandatory treatment, and jail time.
NARRATION: Even one drink can impair reaction time; a designated driver or rideshare
is always the safest option.

SLIDE 19 | Seatbelt and Child Restraint Laws
All states except New Hampshire require adult seatbelt use. Seatbelts reduce the risk
of death in a crash by about 45%. Children must use age- and weight-appropriate car
seats. Infants ride rear-facing until at least age 2 or the maximum weight of the
seat. Older children use forward-facing seats, then booster seats, before transitioning
to seatbelts alone when they fit properly (typically around age 8-12).
NARRATION: Seatbelts save lives; car seats must be installed correctly and children
must remain rear-facing as long as possible.

SLIDE 20 | Distracted Driving
Distracted driving includes any activity that takes your eyes, hands, or attention from
driving. Texting is the most dangerous because it combines all three types of distraction.
At 55 mph, five seconds of looking at your phone covers the length of a football field
with your eyes closed. Most states ban handheld phone use while driving. Eating, adjusting
the radio, and reaching for objects are also common distractions.
NARRATION: No text or call is worth your life; use hands-free devices or pull over
safely if you must use your phone.

SLIDE 21 | Vehicle Safety Checks Before Driving
Before driving, perform a BLOWS check: Brakes (test pedal firmness), Lights (headlights,
tail, brake, and turn signals), Oil (check level when engine is cold), Water (coolant
level), and Safety belts. Also check tire pressure and tread depth, mirrors, windshield
wipers, and fuel level. Report any warning lights on the dashboard to a mechanic promptly.
NARRATION: A quick pre-trip check catches problems before they become dangerous failures
on the road.

SLIDE 22 | What to Do in a Crash
Move your vehicle to the side of the road if it is safe to do so and turn on hazard
lights. Call 911 if anyone is injured. Exchange information with the other driver:
name, address, phone number, driver's license number, insurance company, and policy
number. Do not admit fault at the scene. If a crash involves an unattended vehicle or
property, leave a written note and report it to police.
NARRATION: Stay calm after a crash, prioritize medical help, and never admit fault;
let insurance companies determine responsibility.

SLIDE 23 | Adverse Weather Driving
In rain, reduce speed, increase following distance, and turn on headlights. In fog,
use low beams, not high beams, as high beams reflect back and reduce visibility.
On ice and snow, accelerate and brake gently and increase following distance to eight
to ten seconds. If your vehicle hydroplanes, ease off the gas and steer straight until
tires regain traction. Never use cruise control on wet or icy roads.
NARRATION: Hydroplaning means your tires are riding on water, not the road; gently
ease off the gas and do not brake suddenly.

SLIDE 24 | Night Driving and Headlight Rules
Turn on headlights from 30 minutes after sunset to 30 minutes before sunrise and
whenever visibility is less than 1,000 feet. Use high beams on dark rural roads with
no oncoming traffic; dim them within 500 feet of an oncoming vehicle and 300 feet
when following another vehicle. Dirty headlights and windshields significantly reduce
night visibility; keep them clean.
NARRATION: Most states require headlights whenever wipers are in use; when in doubt,
turn them on.

SLIDE 25 | License Exam Format and Tips
The written permit test typically has 20-50 questions depending on the state, with a
passing score of 70-80%. Questions cover signs, laws, and safe driving practices
directly from the state driver's handbook. Take practice tests on your state's DMV
website. Study road signs visually, not just by name. Read each question completely
before answering; distractor answer choices are designed to catch common mistakes.
NARRATION: Your state's DMV website often has free practice tests that use the same
question style as the real exam; take them until you consistently score above 90%.

SLIDE 26 | Expressway and Highway Driving
Enter expressways by accelerating on the on-ramp to match traffic speed before
merging. Use the left lane for passing only; return to the right lane after passing.
Maintain safe following distances at highway speeds. Exit using the deceleration lane
and signal early. Watch for minimum speed limit signs; driving too slowly on a highway
is a violation and creates a hazard. Always stay alert at highway speeds where reaction
time is critical.
NARRATION: Highway driving requires forward planning; watch far ahead, plan your
exits early, and keep a consistent speed in the right lane.

FACT: The legal BAC limit for adult drivers is 0.08% in all U.S. states.
FACT: Stop signs are always red octagons; they require a complete stop, not a rolling stop.
FACT: Yellow diamond signs are warning signs indicating a hazard ahead.
FACT: The three-second following distance rule should increase in poor weather.
FACT: All traffic in both directions must stop for a school bus with red flashing lights on an undivided road.
FACT: Pedestrians always have the right of way in marked and unmarked crosswalks.
FACT: You must stay at least 15 feet from a fire hydrant when parking.
FACT: Text messaging is the most dangerous distraction because it involves visual, manual, and cognitive distraction.
FACT: Children should remain rear-facing in a car seat until at least age 2.
FACT: Use low beams in fog; high beams reflect off fog and reduce visibility further.
