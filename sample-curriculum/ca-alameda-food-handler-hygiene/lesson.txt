SUMMARY: Handwashing, gloves, and illness reporting for CA food handler card prep. Study aid only — not Alameda County–accredited training. US-generic ServSafe topics remain useful background.

SLIDE 1 | Prep card, not accreditation
This lesson prepares you for California food handler card topics used in Alameda County workplaces. It is not county-accredited training. Follow your employer’s approved course and Alameda County Environmental Health guidance.
NARRATION: This is practice prep only — complete your employer’s approved California food handler course for the real card.

SLIDE 2 | Why food handler cards matter
California requires many food employees to hold a valid food handler card. Local environmental health, including Alameda County, enforces safe practices during inspections.
NARRATION: Keep your card current and follow local inspection expectations every shift.

SLIDE 3 | Handwashing that works
Wash with soap and warm water for at least 20 seconds before handling food, after restroom use, after raw meat, after touching face, hair, or garbage, and when switching tasks. Sanitizer is not a substitute for washing.
NARRATION: Twenty seconds with soap and warm water — every time the task changes.

SLIDE 4 | Gloves done right
Change gloves when torn or dirty, after raw proteins, and before ready-to-eat foods. Wash hands before putting on a new pair.
NARRATION: Dirty gloves spread germs — wash, then glove, then work.

SLIDE 5 | Illness reporting
Report vomiting, diarrhea, jaundice, or sore throat with fever to your manager before working. Diagnosed Salmonella Typhi, Shigella, STEC, hepatitis A, or norovirus require exclusion rules your manager must follow.
NARRATION: When you feel sick, tell your manager before you touch food.

SLIDE 6 | Personal cleanliness
Wear clean clothes and aprons, restrain hair, keep nails short and clean, and avoid jewelry that can trap soil. Do not eat, drink, or smoke in food prep areas except in designated spots.
NARRATION: Clean uniforms and restrained hair are part of food safety, not just appearance.

SLIDE 7 | Ready-to-eat foods
Do not touch ready-to-eat foods with bare hands where rules require barriers — use gloves, tongs, or deli paper as trained.
NARRATION: Ready-to-eat food needs a clean barrier between hands and the plate.

SLIDE 8 | Cuts and bandages
Cover wounds with clean bandages and wear gloves over hand wounds. Stay off the line if you cannot protect food from contamination.
NARRATION: Cover wounds completely before returning to food handling.

SLIDE 9 | Customer allergen basics
Know major allergens — the FDA Big 9 includes sesame. Never guess ingredients; check with the kitchen and prevent cross-contact.
NARRATION: Allergen mistakes can be life-threatening — verify before you serve.
