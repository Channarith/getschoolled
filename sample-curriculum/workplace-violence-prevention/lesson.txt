LESSON: Workplace Violence Prevention
LANGUAGE: en
AUDIENCE: corporate
TRACK: Compliance
LEVEL: Required annual training
ROLE: All employees
DELIVERY: 45–60 min live class
FIT: Everyone who works onsite, remotely, or with the public.
SUMMARY: Recognize warning signs, de-escalate safely, report threats, and follow OSHA-aligned workplace violence policy.

SLIDE 1 | What Is Workplace Violence?
Workplace violence includes physical assaults, threats, intimidation,
stalking, and verbal abuse that occur at work or are connected to work. It can
come from coworkers, customers, patients, intimate partners, or strangers.
OSHA expects employers to identify risks and train employees to prevent and
respond to violence.
NARRATION: Workplace violence includes physical assaults, threats, intimidation, stalking, and verbal abuse that occur at work or are connected to work.

SLIDE 2 | Types of Workplace Violence
Type I involves criminal intent by strangers. Type II involves customers or
clients. Type III involves current or former employees. Type IV involves
personal relationships that spill into the workplace. Knowing the type helps
you choose the right controls, from access control to EAP support.
NARRATION: Type I involves criminal intent by strangers.

SLIDE 3 | Early Warning Signs
Warning signs can include escalating arguments, fixation on grievances,
threats, sudden isolation, or bringing weapons references into conversation.
No single sign proves intent, but patterns of concerning behavior must be
reported. Never investigate alone or confront someone you believe is
dangerous.
NARRATION: Warning signs can include escalating arguments, fixation on grievances, threats, sudden isolation, or bringing weapons references into conversation.

SLIDE 4 | De-escalation Basics
Stay calm, keep distance, speak in short clear sentences, and avoid arguing.
Offer choices when possible and involve a supervisor or security early. If you
feel unsafe, leave the area and call for help. Your goal is safety, not
winning the argument.
NARRATION: Stay calm, keep distance, speak in short clear sentences, and avoid arguing.

SLIDE 5 | Reporting and Support
Report threats, stalking, and concerning behavior through the channels in your
company policy. Document facts: who, what, when, where. Employees who report
in good faith are protected from retaliation. After an incident, follow
emergency procedures and use available counseling resources.
NARRATION: Report threats, stalking, and concerning behavior through the channels in your company policy.

SLIDE 6 | Your Role Every Day
Badge in correctly, do not prop doors, challenge unfamiliar people politely,
and keep valuables secured. Know your exits and panic procedures. Culture
matters: speak up early so small problems do not become crises.
NARRATION: Badge in correctly, do not prop doors, challenge unfamiliar people politely, and keep valuables secured.
