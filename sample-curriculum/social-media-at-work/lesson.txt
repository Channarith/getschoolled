LESSON: Social Media at Work
LANGUAGE: en
AUDIENCE: corporate
TRACK: Compliance
LEVEL: Required annual training
ROLE: All employees
DELIVERY: 30–45 min live class
FIT: Employees who post online about work, customers, or the company brand.
SUMMARY: Protect confidential information, avoid harassment online, and represent the company responsibly.

SLIDE 1 | Why Social Media Policies Matter
Posts can spread instantly to customers, regulators, and journalists. A
careless update can leak trade secrets, harm colleagues, or create legal risk.
Clear rules protect both employees and the brand.
NARRATION: Posts can spread instantly to customers, regulators, and journalists.

SLIDE 2 | Do Not Disclose Confidential Information
Never post unreleased products, financial results, customer data, internal
emails, or screenshots from private systems. If you would not send it to a
competitor, do not post it.
NARRATION: Never post unreleased products, financial results, customer data, internal emails, or screenshots from private systems.

SLIDE 3 | Respect and Harassment
Harassment policies apply online. Do not mock, threaten, or dox coworkers or
customers. Report abusive content that involves work relationships.
NARRATION: Harassment policies apply online.

SLIDE 4 | Speaking for the Company
Unless you are an approved spokesperson, make clear that opinions are your
own. Do not create unofficial accounts that look official. Route media
requests to Communications.
NARRATION: Unless you are an approved spokesperson, make clear that opinions are your own.

SLIDE 5 | Personal vs Professional
Even personal accounts can affect employment when posts involve
discrimination, threats, or confidential work details. Pause before posting
and follow your company social media policy.
NARRATION: Even personal accounts can affect employment when posts involve discrimination, threats, or confidential work details.

FACT: Posts can spread instantly to customers, regulators, and journalists.
FACT: Never post unreleased products, financial results, customer data, internal emails, or screenshots from private systems.
FACT: Harassment policies apply online.
FACT: Unless you are an approved spokesperson, make clear that opinions are your own.
FACT: Even personal accounts can affect employment when posts involve discrimination, threats, or confidential work details.
