LESSON: Security Policies at Work
LANGUAGE: en
AUDIENCE: corporate
TRACK: Privacy
LEVEL: Required annual training
ROLE: All employees
DELIVERY: 40–50 min live class
FIT: Anyone with access to company systems, buildings, or confidential information.
SUMMARY: Acceptable use, badge access, device hygiene, visitor control, and how security policies protect the business.

SLIDE 1 | Why Security Policies Exist
Security policies set clear rules for protecting people, information, systems,
and facilities. They reduce ambiguity when something goes wrong and create a
shared standard across teams. Ignoring them can lead to breaches, fines, or
safety incidents.
NARRATION: Security policies set clear rules for protecting people, information, systems, and facilities.

SLIDE 2 | Acceptable Use
Company devices, email, chat, and cloud tools are for business purposes with
limited personal use as defined by policy. Do not install unapproved software,
store company data on personal drives, or share accounts. If a tool is not
approved, ask IT before using it.
NARRATION: Company devices, email, chat, and cloud tools are for business purposes with limited personal use as defined by policy.

SLIDE 3 | Physical Security
Wear your badge, challenge tailgaters politely, escort visitors, and lock
screens when you leave. Secure cabinets and meeting rooms that hold sensitive
materials. Report lost badges immediately.
NARRATION: Wear your badge, challenge tailgaters politely, escort visitors, and lock screens when you leave.

SLIDE 4 | Clean Desk and Confidentiality
Do not leave printouts, badges, or unlocked devices unattended. Shred
documents with sensitive data. Discuss confidential matters in private spaces
and verify recipients before sending.
NARRATION: Do not leave printouts, badges, or unlocked devices unattended.

SLIDE 5 | Incident Reporting
Report phishing, lost devices, suspicious visitors, and policy exceptions
immediately. Early reporting limits damage. Security teams prefer a false
alarm over a silent breach.
NARRATION: Report phishing, lost devices, suspicious visitors, and policy exceptions immediately.

FACT: Security policies set clear rules for protecting people, information, systems, and facilities.
FACT: Company devices, email, chat, and cloud tools are for business purposes with limited personal use as defined by policy.
FACT: Wear your badge, challenge tailgaters politely, escort visitors, and lock screens when you leave.
FACT: Do not leave printouts, badges, or unlocked devices unattended.
FACT: Report phishing, lost devices, suspicious visitors, and policy exceptions immediately.
