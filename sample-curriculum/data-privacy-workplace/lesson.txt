LESSON: Data Privacy at Work
LANGUAGE: en
AUDIENCE: corporate
TRACK: Privacy
LEVEL: Required annual training
ROLE: All employees
DELIVERY: 40–50 min live class
FIT: Anyone who collects, stores, or shares personal or customer data.
SUMMARY: Minimize data, protect personal information, honor access requests, and report privacy incidents quickly.

SLIDE 1 | What Counts as Personal Data?
Personal data includes names, emails, phone numbers, government IDs, location,
biometrics, and any information that can identify a person alone or combined.
Customer and employee data both count. Treat it as sensitive by default.
NARRATION: Personal data includes names, emails, phone numbers, government IDs, location, biometrics, and any information that can identify a person alone or combined.

SLIDE 2 | Privacy Principles
Collect only what you need, keep it accurate, limit who can access it, and
delete it when no longer required. These principles appear in GDPR, CCPA/CPRA,
and many company policies.
NARRATION: Collect only what you need, keep it accurate, limit who can access it, and delete it when no longer required.

SLIDE 3 | Everyday Habits
Use approved systems, encrypt devices, avoid forwarding personal data to
personal email, and double-check recipients. Mask data in screenshots and
demos.
NARRATION: Use approved systems, encrypt devices, avoid forwarding personal data to personal email, and double-check recipients.

SLIDE 4 | Requests and Rights
Individuals may request access, deletion, or correction of their data. Route
these requests to Privacy or Legal promptly — do not improvise replies.
NARRATION: Individuals may request access, deletion, or correction of their data.

SLIDE 5 | Incident Response
If data is lost, emailed to the wrong person, or exposed, report immediately.
Fast reporting reduces harm and may be legally required.
NARRATION: If data is lost, emailed to the wrong person, or exposed, report immediately.

FACT: Personal data includes names, emails, phone numbers, government IDs, location, biometrics, and any information that can identify a person alone or combined.
FACT: Collect only what you need, keep it accurate, limit who can access it, and delete it when no longer required.
FACT: Use approved systems, encrypt devices, avoid forwarding personal data to personal email, and double-check recipients.
FACT: Individuals may request access, deletion, or correction of their data.
FACT: If data is lost, emailed to the wrong person, or exposed, report immediately.
