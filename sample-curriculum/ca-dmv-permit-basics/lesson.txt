LESSON: California DMV Permit Prep — Rules of the Road (basics)

FIT: Learners preparing for the California DMV knowledge / permit test.
SUMMARY: California-focused right-of-way, speed, signals, and following distance. Study aid only — not a DMV-approved driver education course.

SLIDE 1 | Prep, not a DMV course
This short lesson helps you study for the California knowledge test. It is not DMV-approved driver education. Always use the current California Driver's Handbook as the source of truth and take official practice tests at dmv.ca.gov.
NARRATION: This is certification prep only — confirm every rule in the official California Driver's Handbook.

SLIDE 2 | California learner's permit overview
To get a provisional instruction permit in California you typically need to pass a vision exam and a knowledge test, complete required application steps, and meet age and education requirements. Confirm current rules on dmv.ca.gov before you apply.
NARRATION: Requirements change — verify the latest California permit steps on the DMV website before you go in.

SLIDE 3 | Right-of-way at stops
At a four-way stop, the first vehicle to arrive goes first. If two arrive together, yield to the driver on your right. Yield to pedestrians in marked or unmarked crosswalks and to emergency vehicles with lights and sirens.
NARRATION: When in doubt, yield — especially to pedestrians and emergency vehicles.

SLIDE 4 | California Basic Speed Law
California’s Basic Speed Law says never drive faster than is safe for conditions, even below the posted limit. Common posted limits include 25 mph in business or residential districts unless posted otherwise, and higher freeway limits as posted.
NARRATION: The posted limit is a maximum for ideal conditions — slow down for weather, traffic, and visibility.

SLIDE 5 | Following distance
Use at least a three-second following gap in good conditions. Increase space in rain, fog, night, heavy traffic, or when following large trucks and motorcycles.
NARRATION: Three seconds is a minimum — give yourself more space when conditions are imperfect.

SLIDE 6 | Signals and lane changes
Signal before turns and lane changes. Check mirrors, signal, check your blind spot over your shoulder, then move smoothly. Do not change lanes in an intersection.
NARRATION: Mirrors alone miss blind spots — look over your shoulder every lane change.

SLIDE 7 | Turning and red lights in California
California generally allows a right turn on red after a complete stop unless a sign prohibits it. Yield to pedestrians and cross traffic. Left on red is only allowed from a one-way street onto a one-way street when permitted.
NARRATION: Always stop completely before a right on red, then yield before you turn.

SLIDE 8 | School buses in California
When a school bus displays flashing red lights and a stop signal arm on an undivided roadway, traffic in both directions must stop. On a divided highway, only traffic traveling the same direction as the bus must stop. Stay stopped until the lights stop and the arm is withdrawn.
NARRATION: Failing to stop for a school bus is a serious violation with heavy penalties.

SLIDE 9 | Seat belts and phones
California requires seat belt use. Handheld phone use while driving is restricted — use hands-free or pull over safely. Distracted driving citations and crash risk are high.
NARRATION: Put the phone away — hands-free still needs your attention on the road.

SLIDE 10 | DUI limits in California
For drivers 21 and older, the BAC limit is 0.08 percent. Drivers under 21 are subject to zero-tolerance rules. Commercial drivers have a lower limit. Never drive impaired.
NARRATION: Arrange another ride if you have been drinking or taking impairing medication.

SLIDE 11 | After a crash
If safe, move out of traffic and turn on hazards. Call 911 for injuries. Exchange names, contact, license, and insurance information. Do not argue fault at the scene; report as required.
NARRATION: Stay calm, get help for injuries first, and exchange information without admitting fault.

SLIDE 12 | Study next, then pause
Review signs, sharing the road, and weather driving in the next short lessons. Prefer 15–20 minute blocks with practice quizzes over multi-hour cramming.
NARRATION: Take a short break or come back later — spaced practice beats marathon study sessions.
