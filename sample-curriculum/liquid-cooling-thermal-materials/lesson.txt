LESSON: Liquid Cooling & Thermal Materials Safety
LANGUAGE: en
AUDIENCE: corporate
TRACK: Safety
LEVEL: Role-based required
ROLE: Lab, facilities, hardware teams
DELIVERY: 45–60 min live class
FIT: Anyone installing, maintaining, or disposing of liquid cooling loops and thermal interface materials.
SUMMARY: Spill response, PPE, chemical handling, electrical isolation, and safe work around coolants and thermal materials.

SLIDE 1 | Why Thermal Systems Need Special Care
Liquid cooling and thermal interface materials improve performance but
introduce chemical, electrical, and slip hazards. Coolants can be conductive,
irritating, or environmentally regulated. Treat every maintenance job as a
controlled procedure.
NARRATION: Liquid cooling and thermal interface materials improve performance but introduce chemical, electrical, and slip hazards.

SLIDE 2 | Know Your Materials
Identify coolants, dielectric fluids, pastes, pads, and solvents on the SDS
before use. Note flash points, PPE, incompatible materials, and disposal
rules. Never mix unknown fluids.
NARRATION: Identify coolants, dielectric fluids, pastes, pads, and solvents on the SDS before use.

SLIDE 3 | Electrical Isolation First
Power down and lock out equipment before opening loops or heat sinks near
energized gear. Confirm residual energy is released. Liquids plus live power
create shock and short risks.
NARRATION: Power down and lock out equipment before opening loops or heat sinks near energized gear.

SLIDE 4 | Spill and Leak Response
Contain spills immediately, keep people clear, wear required PPE, and follow
the SDS cleanup method. Report leaks that reach drains. Dispose of
contaminated materials as hazardous waste when required.
NARRATION: Contain spills immediately, keep people clear, wear required PPE, and follow the SDS cleanup method.

SLIDE 5 | Safe Handling Habits
Use drip trays, labeled containers, and secondary containment. Inspect hoses
and fittings. Wash hands after handling thermal materials and never eat in the
work area.
NARRATION: Use drip trays, labeled containers, and secondary containment.

FACT: Liquid cooling and thermal interface materials improve performance but introduce chemical, electrical, and slip hazards.
FACT: Identify coolants, dielectric fluids, pastes, pads, and solvents on the SDS before use.
FACT: Power down and lock out equipment before opening loops or heat sinks near energized gear.
FACT: Contain spills immediately, keep people clear, wear required PPE, and follow the SDS cleanup method.
FACT: Use drip trays, labeled containers, and secondary containment.
