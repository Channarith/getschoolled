Cyber Incident Response Drills
==============================

Containment without destruction — ransomware, breach, and insider scenarios.
Use track_id ``cyber_ir`` or domain ``cybersecurity``. This drill follows the
NIST incident-response lifecycle: preparation, detection and analysis,
containment, eradication, recovery, and post-incident review.

Module 1 — Preparation before anything breaks
----------------------------------------------
The response you can execute is decided long before the alert fires. Keep an
up-to-date asset inventory, an on-call rotation with clear roles, offline and
tested backups, and a communication plan with pre-approved holding statements.
Store the incident-response plan somewhere reachable even if the network is
down, and rehearse it so muscle memory takes over under stress.

Module 2 — Detection and triage
-------------------------------
Not every alert is an incident. Triage confirms whether something real is
happening, how urgent it is, and which systems are affected. Correlate signals
from endpoint detection, firewall and identity logs, and user reports; assign a
severity; and open a timeline you will append to for the rest of the incident.
Getting severity right routes the right people in without crying wolf.

Module 3 — Isolate to slow the spread
-------------------------------------
Segment affected hosts to stop lateral movement — disable network access,
revoke suspect credentials, and block command-and-control domains. Avoid wiping
disks before evidence is preserved unless business survival requires it: a
rebuilt machine is a destroyed witness. Prefer isolation over shutdown so
volatile memory and live indicators remain available to investigators.

Module 4 — Preserve evidence
----------------------------
Before you change a compromised system, capture what it can tell you: memory
images, disk snapshots, and relevant logs, each with hashes and a chain of
custody. Evidence supports eradication decisions, insurance and legal needs, and
prosecution. Work from copies; never investigate on the only original.

Module 5 — Verify before you trust
----------------------------------
CEO-fraud wire requests and polished phishing pages are engineered to feel
urgent. Out-of-band verification — a call to a known number, not a reply to the
email — is not optional. Assume attacker-controlled channels during an incident,
and confirm identities and instructions through a second, independent path.

Module 6 — Eradicate the root cause
-----------------------------------
Removing the malware is not the same as removing the way in. Identify and close
the initial access vector — the unpatched service, the stolen credential, the
malicious rule — and remove persistence mechanisms across every affected host.
If you skip this, the attacker simply returns through the same door.

Module 7 — Recover safely
-------------------------
Restore from known-good backups, rebuild rather than clean where trust is in
doubt, and bring systems back in a monitored, staged way. Watch closely for
re-infection, rotate all potentially exposed secrets, and only declare recovery
once services are verified healthy and clean.

Module 8 — Communicate facts only
---------------------------------
Leadership, customers, and PR will want answers immediately. Separate confirmed
facts from hypotheses, give a single source of truth, and update on a cadence.
Speculation in public becomes tomorrow's correction; say what you know, what you
are doing, and when you will update next.

Module 9 — Legal and notification duties
-----------------------------------------
Many breaches carry deadlines — regulators, affected individuals, and partners
may need timely notice, and some jurisdictions require it within days. Involve
legal and privacy early, log every decision, and understand your obligations
before the clock, not after. Missed notifications often cost more than the
incident.

Module 10 — Foresight: what spreads in fifteen minutes
------------------------------------------------------
Map the likely lateral-movement paths before declaring containment complete.
Ask what an attacker reaches next from the beachhead you found — shared
credentials, trust relationships, backups — and cut those paths proactively
rather than chasing the intrusion one host at a time.

Module 11 — Post-incident review
--------------------------------
When the fire is out, run a blameless review while memory is fresh. What
happened, what worked, what slowed you down, and which fixes prevent a repeat?
Turn findings into concrete actions with owners and dates, and feed them back
into preparation so the next response is faster.

Module 12 — Tabletop drills
---------------------------
Practice on calm days. Walk the team through ransomware, data-breach, and
insider scenarios; time your detection-to-containment; and surface the gaps —
missing contacts, stale backups, unclear authority — before a real adversary
does. Rehearsed teams recover measurably faster.
