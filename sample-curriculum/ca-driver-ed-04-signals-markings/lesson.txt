SLIDE 1 | Steady green
Go only if the intersection is clear. A green light means proceed if the way is clear and you yield to pedestrians still crossing.
NARRATION: A green light means proceed if the way is clear and you yield to pedestrians still crossing.

SLIDE 2 | Steady yellow
Stop if you can do so safely. Yellow means the light is changing to red — stop before the intersection if you can stop safely.
NARRATION: Yellow means the light is changing to red — stop before the intersection if you can stop safely.

SLIDE 3 | Steady red
Full stop; turns only when allowed. Red means stop. Right on red is allowed in California after a full stop unless signed otherwise.
NARRATION: Red means stop. Right on red is allowed in California after a full stop unless signed otherwise.

SLIDE 4 | Flashing red
Treat like a stop sign. A flashing red signal requires a complete stop, then proceed when safe — like a stop sign.
NARRATION: A flashing red signal requires a complete stop, then proceed when safe — like a stop sign.

SLIDE 5 | Flashing yellow
Proceed with caution. Flashing yellow means slow down and proceed carefully through the intersection.
NARRATION: Flashing yellow means slow down and proceed carefully through the intersection.

SLIDE 6 | Green arrow
Protected turn — still scan. A green arrow gives a protected turn, but still watch for pedestrians and red-light runners.
NARRATION: A green arrow gives a protected turn, but still watch for pedestrians and red-light runners.

SLIDE 7 | Yellow center lines
Separate opposite directions. Yellow lines separate traffic moving in opposite directions.
NARRATION: Yellow lines separate traffic moving in opposite directions.

SLIDE 8 | White lane lines
Separate same-direction lanes. White lines separate lanes traveling in the same direction.
NARRATION: White lines separate lanes traveling in the same direction.

SLIDE 9 | Solid vs broken
Solid means do not cross for passing. A solid line on your side means do not pass or change lanes across it.
NARRATION: A solid line on your side means do not pass or change lanes across it.

SLIDE 10 | Crosswalk markings
Yield to people in the crosswalk. Marked and unmarked crosswalks require you to yield to pedestrians.
NARRATION: Marked and unmarked crosswalks require you to yield to pedestrians.

FACT: Always verify rules in the current California Driver's Handbook.
